### Project Overview

The proposed product is a shade-optimized walking route planning tool for urban pedestrians in Quezon City, particularly workers and other pedestrians who walk to nearby locations for lunch, errands, or daily activities. The problem is that pedestrians currently rely on personal knowledge, visual observation, umbrellas, familiar routes, or reactive route changes to avoid direct sunlight, making it difficult to determine how much of a complete walking route will be exposed to sunlight at a particular time. The proposed product will provide an interactive map that allows users to specify a starting location, destination, and intended travel time, then view and compare walking routes based on estimated direct sunlight and shaded portions, together with route distance and estimated walking duration. The project will be developed as a prototype operating within a limited geographic area of Quezon City and will focus specifically on outdoor direct sunlight exposure; it will not address indoor routes, general walkability, overall thermal comfort, heat stress, or health risks.

### Needs Assessment

The initial statement for this case is that an urban pedestrian in Quezon City needs to travel on foot during hot periods of the day while minimizing exposure to direct sunlight.

| Part of needs identification | Description for this case |
| :--- | :--- |
| Stakeholders | Urban pedestrians, particularly workers who walk to nearby locations for lunch, errands, or other daily activities. |
| Task and decision | Walk from one location to another within the city during daily activities while attempting to limit exposure to direct sunlight. |
| Object or data | The user examines the walking environment and route, particularly areas that are shaded or exposed to direct sunlight. |
| Working conditions | The pedestrian is walking through an urban environment in Quezon City, where routes may contain a mixture of shaded and sun-exposed areas. Shade may be provided by buildings, covered walkways, or other structures. The need is particularly relevant around noon and during the afternoon when direct sunlight can be intense. |
| Duration and handling | The pedestrian may spend several minutes walking between locations. During the trip, they may move repeatedly between shaded and sun-exposed sections. The amount of shade available can change throughout the day as the position of the sun changes. |
| Current method and workaround | The pedestrian relies on personal knowledge and visual observation to select shaded areas. Common workarounds include carrying an umbrella, walking on the shaded side of a street, selecting familiar routes with known shade, changing routes while walking, or using public/private transportation instead of walking. |
| Problem with the current method | These methods are largely reactive and do not provide a systematic way to determine how much direct sunlight a complete route will receive at a particular time. Personal knowledge may also be limited in unfamiliar areas, and shade conditions change throughout the day as the sun moves, making observations from another time of day unreliable. |
| Consequences | The pedestrian may choose a route with greater direct sunlight exposure than necessary, particularly when unfamiliar with the area or when shade conditions differ from previous observations. |
| Evidence | Based on personal observations and preference, shaded sides of streets and routes with known shade are often preferred during hot periods. |
| User need | An urban pedestrian in Quezon City needs to travel on foot while minimizing exposure to direct sunlight, with information that helps them identify and compare routes based on sunlight and shade conditions at their intended travel time. |

### Product Definition and Scope

The product will be a prototype shade-optimized walking route planning tool for urban pedestrians in Quezon City. It will provide an interactive map that allows users to specify a starting location, destination, and intended travel time, then view walking routes with information about their expected direct sunlight and shaded portions. The product will support pedestrians in comparing available routes and selecting a route that reduces direct sunlight exposure while also considering route characteristics such as distance and estimated walking duration.

The project will cover the development of a prototype that uses available geographic and environmental data, such as roads, buildings, and sun-position information, to estimate and visualize direct sunlight exposure along outdoor walking routes. It will include route visualization, distinction between shaded and sun-exposed sections, route comparison, and relevant route information such as distance and estimated walking duration. The project will not cover indoor walking routes, general walkability factors such as sidewalk quality, accessibility, traffic safety, or cleanliness, or prediction of overall thermal comfort, heat stress, or health risks. The scope is constrained by the available public datasets, their geographic coverage and accuracy, the processing capabilities of the available laptop, and the two-week development timeline. The availability and quality of building-height remain unresolved and may affect the accuracy and final scope of the prototype.
